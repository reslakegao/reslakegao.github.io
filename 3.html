T / F · Decisions

（Thinking vs Feeling）

What is T / F？

T（Thinking）：It is more inclined to judge by logic, analysis and principles. Pay attention to right and wrong, standards and objective consistency.

F（Feeling）：I am more inclined to judge by values, relationships and feelings. Pay attention to whether it is harmonious, fair and whether others are taken care of

This is not "cold-blooded vs. sentimental", but more common starting points for decision-making

Common manifestations

Strengths T F

T · Key Points

  • Analyze the logic and deduce the plan
  • Maintain objectivity and consistency based on principles
  • Quickly locate vulnerabilities and boundaries

F · Key Points

  • Understand the atmosphere and the scope of influence
  • Coordinate relations and promote consensus
  • Balance fairness and acceptability
Risks T F

T · Possible bias

  • Plain language seems inhumane
  • Ignore the human hearts and costs at the time of implementation
  • The priority of standards leads to a low communication temperature

F · Possible bias

  • Excessive accommodation makes it difficult to make decisions
  • Make passive concessions in complex games
  • The ambiguity of standards leads to wavering in implementation
Tips T F

For T

  • Beyond logic, add a sentence: "How would people feel?"
  • When reviewing, check the tone and rhythm of communication
  • Make a "stakeholder map" before making key decisions

For F

  • Beyond the relationship, add a question: "What is the standard?"
  • Write down three measurable criteria for judgment
  • Transform "who to take care of" into clear rules

🧩 Team discussion ›

T: Directly raise the issue and logical loopholes;
F: First, confirm the emotions and relationships, and then organize the opinions.

📚 Course/Task selection ›

T: More emphasis is placed on verifiable learning benefits and difficulty gradients;
F: I care more about the mentor's style, the class atmosphere and the experience of cooperation.

🤝 resolve conflict ›

T: First, clarify the boundaries between facts and responsibilities;
F: First, soothe the emotions, and then seek a transitional solution that is acceptable to all parties.

Totally

T/F is not about "cold rationality vs. being emotional", but rather about "which type of standard to refer to first". Putting both perspectives on the table will make decisions more stable.
